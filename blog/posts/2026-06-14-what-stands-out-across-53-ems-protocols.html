We Compared 53 EMS Protocols From Across the Country. This Is What We Found.

By Jaden • June 14, 2026 • 6 min read • — views

Each was uploaded to ProtoQuiz by an EMT or medic studying their own agency's rules. An AI model read them page by page (about 47 million tokens over six days). Every fact below is page-cited and fact-checked.

Five interesting findings

Maple syrup is an approved oral glucose in Vermont. Vermont EMS Statewide Protocols

Naloxone is banned in cardiac arrest, in red text. San Diego County, against the common "it won't hurt" habit. San Diego County EMS, p.57

A gaze test instead of FAST. Alabama's EMSA stroke scale uses gaze tracking, not facial droop. Alabama Statewide EMS Protocols, p.206

Field thrombolytics for STEMI. St John New Zealand gives tenecteplase plus heparin 5000 U on a standing order; no US system here does. St John New Zealand, p.62

Field perimortem cesarean. Mayo Clinic Ambulance (critical-care transport) authorizes it at 4 minutes without ROSC, ≥20 weeks. Mayo Clinic Ambulance, p.255

Together: 5,556 medication entries, 2,470 algorithms and 1,393 procedures.

What varies most

  • Formularies run from 4 drugs (Davidson County, NC) to 177 (Kentucky).
  • Starting doses. Most start adenosine at 6 mg; Boulder, Denver Metro, San Antonio and West Palm Beach start at 12. TXA splits between 1 g and 2 g.
  • D10 is replacing D50. Santa Cruz and LA County use D10 for all hypoglycemia (p.228, p.25).
  • Arrest. Berrien County uses lidocaine over amiodarone (p.91). Vasopressin survives in Kentucky and Miami (p.54). San Diego and San Antonio route refractory arrests to ECMO (p.61, p.107). Alameda allows termination only with 2 or fewer shocks (p.92).
  • Stroke. Almost every system uses a different LVO scale; thrombectomy windows run from 3.5 to 24 hours (p.136).
  • Procedures. About a third carry whole blood. Thurston County (WA) authorizes POCUS-guided pericardiocentesis (p.211). Massachusetts carries field amputation (p.143).

Scope of practice

The National EMS Scope of Practice Model is the baseline behind the NREMT. Real systems treat it as a floor, a ceiling or a suggestion:

New York EMTs give oral moxifloxacin for open fractures (p.86). West Virginia AEMTs access PICC lines (p.188). Alabama makes RSI Critical Care only (p.189). Alberta tops out at EMT, with no intubation at all (p.7).

How we did this

An AI model on a Raspberry Pi read each PDF in roughly 20-page chunks, pulling the same fields every time. Three separate AI skeptic agents then re-checked each standout against the cited page; it made the cut only if at least two confirmed it.

Why this matters for studying

For most of prehospital care there is no single right answer across these 53 systems. NREMT material teaches the conventional version; your agency's protocol is what holds up on a call.
