Medicine Mode Is Live: A Living Textbook for EMS

By Jaden • August 5, 2026

Back in June, at the end of the NREMT prep announcement, I teased the next thing I was building: a way to stay current on the actual medicine behind what we do on the truck. Not the protocol steps - the reason for the steps, the pathophysiology, and the evidence underneath it all.

It's done. Medicine Mode is live in ProtoQuiz, in beta, and I genuinely believe there is nothing else like it in EMS.

The shortest way I can describe it: it's the textbook you never got around to reading - written in plain language, cited to real medical literature, built to let you go as deep as your curiosity takes you, and unlike a textbook, it doesn't go stale.

The Problem It Solves

For most of us, the classroom was years ago. Medicine wasn't done changing when we graduated - guidelines get revised, drugs fall in and out of favor, the research behind a treatment shifts. Keeping up with that while working full shifts is genuinely hard, and there's no tool built for it. The result is a lot of providers running protocols without really knowing the evidence underneath them.

Protocols tell you what to do. Medicine Mode is about why - what's actually happening in the patient's body, why we treat it the way we do, and what the research says. That's the difference between following a checklist and practicing medicine. EMS is a profession, and this is the part of the profession that's been hardest to keep up with.

What's In It

742 topics at launch, covering the conditions you actually see on the truck: cardiac, respiratory, toxicology, trauma, neuro, OB, environmental, psych, and more. Each topic is a full narrative, not a flashcard:

  • Pathophysiology in plain language. What is actually going wrong in the body, explained the way a good instructor would - not the way a 1,400-page textbook does.
  • The evidence behind the treatment. Not "because the protocol says so" - the actual research. Every topic is backed by citations to real, current medical literature. There are over 4,400 references across the library.
  • 800+ images and diagrams where a picture teaches faster than a paragraph - ECGs, anatomy, mechanisms.
  • A built-in glossary. Tap any term you don't recognize and get the definition right there, often with its own image. No leaving the page, no losing your spot.
  • A case vignette on every topic. You don't just read - each topic opens with a case that makes you commit to an answer first. Reading feels like learning; getting tested proves it.

Built to Rabbit-Hole

This is the part I'm proudest of. Every topic is layered. Stay at the quick answer when you just need the answer - or keep peeling: why do we treat it this way, what's actually killing the patient, what does the research say. Tap a glossary term, follow it somewhere you didn't expect, nerd out at 2 a.m. between calls. The depth is there when you want it and out of your way when you don't.

And it doesn't stop at handoff. Most of us wheel a patient through the ER doors and never find out what happened next. Medicine Mode follows the case into the hospital - what the ER does, what the ICU course looks like, how it tends to turn out. After years of never getting to see the second half of the story, you finally do.

Work through a topic's layers and it's marked mastered. There's a streak, and your progress is tracked across the library - but none of it is homework. It's built for curiosity first.

Why "Living Textbook" Isn't Just a Tagline

A textbook is finished the day it's printed, and it starts going out of date the day after. Medicine Mode isn't like that. The library grows in waves - new topics, deeper layers on existing ones - and when the evidence behind a treatment shifts, the topic gets revised. You don't buy a new edition; the current one just gets better.

It also gets better because of you. Every topic has a feedback bar at the bottom. If an explanation doesn't land, if something reads wrong, if there's a topic you want covered - tell me, right there in the app. That feedback directly shapes what gets written and revised next. That's what "living" means: the content moves with the medicine and with the people using it.

And here's the part I keep coming back to: as far as I can tell, nothing else works this way - not just in EMS, but anywhere in medicine. I've looked across emergency medicine, nursing, and physician education, and every tool falls into one of the same three buckets. The prep apps quiz you for a test and stop there. The textbooks - EMS, nursing, or medical school - are frozen the day they're printed. The clinical references that do stay current are lookup tools for hospital clinicians, priced like it, and built to answer a question at the bedside, not to actually teach you the medicine underneath. A living, layered, evidence-cited textbook that teaches, quizzes you, follows the patient from dispatch to ICU, and keeps revising itself as the evidence moves - I haven't found that anywhere, for any provider level. That it exists first for prehospital providers, the people every other tool serves last, is the part I'm proudest of. It's the thing I most wanted to exist when I was coming up.

The Honest Fine Print

Two things, because I've always written these posts straight.

First: it's a beta. The library is big, but some topics go deeper than others, and you will find rough edges. That's exactly why the feedback bar exists - the fastest way this gets great is you using it and telling me where it falls short.

Second: Medicine Mode is part of Premium during the beta. The core of ProtoQuiz - protocol quizzes, scenarios, the full NREMT bank - stays free, same as always. But this library took months to build and keeping it current is real, ongoing work, and Premium is what funds that. If you're on the free tier you'll see the Medicine card in the app and can jump in with an upgrade.

And the standing rule, which matters more here than anywhere else in the app: your local protocol always wins. Medicine Mode teaches you the why behind the medicine - it is not medical direction, and it never overrides what your agency and your medical director tell you to do.

Go Get Lost In It

Open ProtoQuiz and tap the Medicine card - the one with the beta pill on it. Pick whatever you got toned out for last shift and see how deep it goes. My honest suggestion: start with a call that didn't sit right with you. Reading the full story of that patient - pathophys to ER to ICU - is the moment this thing clicks.

Then hit the feedback bar and tell me what you think. This one is personal to me, and I want to know if it lands.
