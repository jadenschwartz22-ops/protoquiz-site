I Built ProtoQuiz for Paramedic School. It's Time to Run It at Your Agency.

By Jaden • May 16, 2026

Update, Sept 2026: The pilot program in this post has closed. For how the agency product works today and current pricing, see ProtoQuiz for Agencies.

ProtoQuiz started as the tool I needed for myself. I'm a working paramedic. I built it to get through school, and I still use it on shift.

The knowledge a provider has to be rock-solid on - pharmacology, procedures, your agency's protocols - is the kind where 80% confident isn't good enough. There are tools for NREMT prep, for trivia, for CE hours. Nothing drilled the scope-specific, drug-by-drug, page-cited knowledge you reach for on a call. So I built it, and now there's a version for agencies: your protocols, your providers, your dashboard, on the same engine.

What It Does for the People on Shift

Providers who use it know your protocols - not "read the PDF three months ago," but active recall on day three, day seventeen and day forty. Which drug, which dose, which decision point, which cert level, and where it lives in your document.

That fluency is what lets a medic deviate intelligently when a call demands it. Protocols are a floor, not a ceiling. The medic who has the formulary internalized is the one who makes the right call when the patient does something the protocol doesn't quite cover.

What It Does for the Org

  • Faster onboarding. New hires do real reps on day one, and FTOs spend their time on judgment and hands-on technique instead of the formulary.
  • Fewer knowledge gaps. Protocol mistakes are mostly knowledge mistakes, and steady, low-friction reps close the gap before it becomes a QA case.
  • Optional competition between crews, which pulls everyone up.
  • Earlier QA signal. The training officer dashboard shows weak protocols and weak providers by name, topic and month. When someone needs remediation, you know what to assign, the system records that they drilled it, and you have a dated record of the whole loop.
  • Everything is auditable. Attempts, completions, assignments and mastery are all logged, and CSV export is one click.
