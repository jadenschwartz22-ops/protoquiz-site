Early release. Data building nightly.

Prehospital care varies from agency to agency, and nobody could see how.

Protocols live in thousands of separate PDFs, so the differences between them have never been readable in one place. The United States EMS Protocol Census reads what agencies publish and turns it into a versioned public record of the drugs, doses and routes they carry, so you can compare agencies across the country. It rebuilds itself every night from the agencies' own documents.

234 protocols207 agencies145 agency pages1,674 documents read363 waiting to be matched to an agency25,951 dosesas of 2026-09-13

Why it matters

  • A medic who changes agencies relearns every dose. The drug is the same and the number is different, and until now there was no way to see which agencies differ or by how much.
  • A medical director revising a protocol has no benchmark. Writing the next version means guessing at what everyone else does. The census shows what the rest of the country actually carries, with the documents behind it.
  • Researchers have never had the dataset. There has been no denominator for prehospital medicine, so questions about how care varies could not be asked, let alone answered.
  • Arguing for EMS pay, training and staffing takes evidence. Anecdote loses those arguments. Numbers that anyone can check and cite do better.
  • It updates every night, and agencies decide whether they are in it. New documents are read and revisions become new versions with the old one kept in the history. Send a public URL to be listed, or ask to be removed and it comes down the same day, no reason needed.
