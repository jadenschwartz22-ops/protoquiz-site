State of US EMS Protocols, Q3 2026

8 doses where US EMS protocols vary most, widest first — some spread far apart, some barely at all. Every figure is drawn from agencies' own published documents, one value per protocol, as of 2026-09-20.

How these were chosen

Nothing here was picked by hand. Every comparable group in the census was ranked by spread — the width of the middle half of protocols, as a fraction of the median — among groups with at least 15 published protocols, and the top 8 are below. The floor stepped down from 15 to 10 to 8 until 6 groups cleared it; this edition used 15.

This edition names no agencies. The figures are aggregate distributions with their counts; individual agencies' protocols are published on their own census pages. How the census is built covers what is measured, what is not, and why no dose-level accuracy number is published.

1. Naloxone for Opioid overdose

Across 142 published protocols, the adult naloxone dose for opioid overdose has a median of 1 mg, with the middle half between 0.4 and 2 mg. The full published range runs 0.4 to 2 mg.

United States EMS Protocol Census, n=142 protocols from 122 named agencies / 32 states, updated Sep 2026

What these numbers are not

They are a description of what agencies have published, not a recommendation and not a judgment about which protocol is right. A wide spread can mean genuine clinical disagreement, different patient populations, different transport times, or a difference in how a dose is written down — this data cannot tell those apart.

Dose-level extraction accuracy has not been measured, and no accuracy figure is published anywhere on this site. Entries under outlier review are excluded from every number here.
