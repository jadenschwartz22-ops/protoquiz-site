Who can stop a code?

In Michigan, every agency we read requires a call to a physician before stopping a resuscitation. In Utah, none does. Whether a paramedic can call it on their own depends on where they work, and often on which county.

We read the written protocols of 125 agencies in 37 states: 105 state a clear rule on medical control, and 102 of those have a known state for the map and table.

38%require a call to medical control every time
53%require it only in some cases
9%let the provider stop under a standing order

Do they honor a POLST?

Of 102 agencies with a DNR protocol, 95% honor an out-of-hospital DNR. POLST and MOLST forms: 62 accept them and 1 does not. The rest say nothing or are ambiguous, so 61% is a floor.

How this was read. A model reads each protocol twice at temperature zero. A value is kept only if both reads agree and the supporting sentence is found verbatim on the page; otherwise the field is held back. Hand-graded on 109 rows. Five agencies whose quoted sentence did not settle the question were checked by hand against the protocol text: four let providers stop on their own authority and one requires a call to medical control, which is how they are counted. Each agency counts once, using its current document. Not yet published: transport timing, rhythm criterion, exclusions, the unwitnessed no-shock rule.
